      Dates. City dates come from the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) observation trees once recorded, otherwise from the Japan Meteorological Corporation (JMC) forecast. Spot dates are JMC forecasts. Averages are the normal dates published with the forecast. For autumn leaves, JMC gives only the difference from normal.

      Okinawa, Amami and five Hokkaido cities are not in the JMC forecast. Their dates are this year's JMA observations. Okinawa and Amami watch the Taiwan cherry, which blooms in winter; most of Hokkaido watches the Sargent cherry.

      Maple and ginkgo. JMC forecasts both per city, and they turn at different times. Most forecast spots are maple; the ginkgo colour at maple and mixed spots is estimated by shifting each spot's maple dates by the nearby cities' ginkgo-to-maple gap. Ginkgo trees stand only at ginkgo and mixed spots. Three cities have no ginkgo forecast.

      Snow on high peaks is a seasonal tint by month, not an observation: white above about 2,000 m from December to February, rising to about 2,800 m in May, none from June to September.

      Shading. Colour between spots is estimated from the six nearest spots and cities. It fades out 28 km from the nearest one, so open sea and empty mountains stay plain. Each group of trees stands on a real forecast spot.

      Front speed is how fast the estimated arrival line moves, averaged over a week.

      Year by year. JMA phenological observation tables, 1953 onward (Government of Japan Standard Terms of Use). Hollow dots are years JMA watched a stand-in species. Autumn history uses JMA maple colouring (kaede) or ginkgo yellowing (icho). The dashed line is a straight-line fit; the solid line is a 10-year running mean. This year's forecast, when not yet observed, is a hollow ring.
